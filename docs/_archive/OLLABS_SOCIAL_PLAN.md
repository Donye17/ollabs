# Ollabs — Social Media Plan

*August 1 to October 31, 2026. Slots into [OLLABS_90_DAY_PLAN.md](./OLLABS_90_DAY_PLAN.md).*

---

## The honest read before anything else

**Social is not your growth channel. It is the credibility layer that makes your growth channels convert.**

Your growth already comes from three places, and none of them is a feed:

1. **SEO.** A Brazilian healthcare co-op and a Philadelphia event both found you with zero promotion.
2. **The campaign link itself.** Every campaign is distribution. One organizer reaches their whole list.
3. **Direct outreach.** Your 90-day plan calls for contacting 10 orgs in September and 25 before October. That is the highest-leverage work in the quarter.

An Ollabs Instagram account posting frames to 60 followers moves none of those numbers. But when you DM a nonprofit comms director in September, the first thing she does is click your profile. If it is empty, you are a stranger with a link. If it has a dozen real posts and three live campaigns, you are a real product she can bring to her boss.

So the job of social this quarter is narrow and worth doing well:

> Make Ollabs look real, active, and safe to say yes to, so that outreach and SEO convert better.

Everything below is built for that. If you only have ninety minutes a week, this plan still works.

## Channel decision

You have no accounts yet, which is an advantage. You get to skip the ones that would waste your time.

| Channel | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | **Primary. Set up first.** | Your buyers are here. Nonprofit comms staff, university advancement teams, HR and culture leads, event organizers. It is also the only channel where a DM from a stranger is normal behavior. |
| **Instagram** | **Secondary. Set up week 2.** | This is your proof surface. Frames are visual, and it is where you point anyone who asks "what does it look like." Low cadence, high polish. |
| **TikTok / Reels** | **Skip this quarter.** | Highest production cost per post and the audience skews away from people with a campaign budget. Revisit in November if you have time you do not have now. |
| **X / Threads** | **Skip, with one exception.** | Not worth a standing cadence. If you want a build-in-public outlet, do it on your personal LinkedIn instead, where the same posts reach people who might actually run a campaign. |

**Two profiles, not one.** An Ollabs company page on LinkedIn (for legitimacy, the link in DMs, and the logo next to your name) and your personal profile (where the reach actually is). Company pages get almost no organic distribution. Personal posts do. Post from you, reshare to the page.

### Setup checklist (do this in week 1, one sitting)

- [ ] LinkedIn company page: Ollabs. Logo is `brand/ollabs-logo-white.png` on ink, or the blue ring alone as the square avatar.
- [ ] Tagline: **Rally your people, one link.**
- [ ] About section: the homepage paragraph, unchanged. It is already in voice.
- [ ] Link to ollabs.studio with a UTM: `?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=profile`
- [ ] Instagram: `@ollabs` or nearest available. Bio = tagline + "Free, no ads, no signup." + link.
- [ ] Add Ollabs to your personal LinkedIn as current experience. This is what makes your DMs land as a founder rather than a spammer.
- [ ] Grab the handle on TikTok and X even though you will not post. Costs five minutes, prevents a squatter.

## The minimum viable version

This is the floor. Everything beyond it is marked optional.

**90 minutes a week, split into three sessions:**

| When | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Sunday | 60 min | Batch. Make the week's assets and write the week's posts. Schedule them. |
| Wednesday | 15 min | Reply to every comment. Send 3 outreach DMs. |
| Friday | 15 min | Reply again. Note anything worth posting next week. |

**Cadence at the floor: 2 posts a week on LinkedIn, 1 on Instagram.** That is 26 LinkedIn posts and 13 Instagram posts across the quarter. It is enough to look alive, which is the entire objective. Do not start at five a week. You will stop in three weeks and a dead feed is worse than a small one.

*Optional, if you find 3 to 5 hours:* add a second Instagram post, and turn one LinkedIn post a week into a short screen recording of the editor. The 15-second "upload photo, frame appears, download" clip is your single most persuasive asset and you do not have one yet.

## Four content pillars

Rotate these. Never post two of the same type back to back.

### 1. The frame drop (visual proof)
A designed frame for an upcoming awareness day, shown on a real photo, with the campaign link. You already have 24 frames built and 37 SEO campaigns seeded, so most of this is publishing work rather than creation work.

*Ratio: about 40% of posts.*

### 2. Campaign spotlight (social proof)
A real campaign and its real number. "A healthcare co-op in Brazil found Ollabs on their own and put a frame on 58 people." This is the most convincing thing you have and you are currently telling nobody.

**Ask permission first.** Your launch plan already flags this for the case study, and the same applies to a post. A short message to the organizer asking if you can feature them costs nothing and usually gets a yes.

*Ratio: about 20%.*

### 3. The position (why Ollabs)
The ad-free promise, stated plainly and repeatedly. Your supporters will never pay, never see an ad, and never get a watermark. Also the honest comparison work you already published at `/vs/twibbonize`, including the part where you say when Twibbon is the better pick. That honesty is a differentiator, so use it in the feed and not just on the site.

*Ratio: about 20%.*

### 4. Build in public (founder voice)
Your personal profile only. What you shipped, what broke, what you learned. The organizer email loop, the migration that dropped thirteen tables, the decision to charge organizations instead of supporters. This pillar costs the least to produce because you are already doing the work.

*Ratio: about 20%.*

## Month by month

Each month has one primary job. The other pillars keep running underneath at low volume.

### August — primary job: exist and look real

You are not selling yet. You are building the profile that September's outreach will land on.

| Week | Focus | Posts |
|---|---|---|
| Aug 3-9 | Setup + first posts | **1.** Founder intro: why you built Ollabs, the Twibbon frustration, what you are trying to make. **2.** Frame drop: show three of the 24 designed frames, explain the design thinking briefly. |
| Aug 10-16 | Nonprofit Day runway | **3.** National Nonprofit Day is Mon Aug 17. Post the frame and link on Aug 12, five days ahead, because organizers plan ahead and supporters do not. **4.** Position post: "no ads, ever, and here is how we plan to pay for it." |
| Aug 17-23 | Nonprofit Day live | **5.** Day-of post, Mon Aug 17. **6.** Build in public: what the organizer email loop does and why you built it. |
| Aug 24-31 | Proof | **7.** Campaign spotlight, permission pending. **8.** Frame drop: preview the September set, suicide prevention and childhood cancer, so orgs planning September see them in time. |

**August exit criteria:** both profiles live, 8 posts up, Nonprofit Day run end to end as a real test of the new email flow.

### September — primary job: recruit organizers

Social stops being broadcast and starts being support fire for outreach. Your 90-day plan says contact 10 organizations this month. Social is what makes those messages work.

- **Post the frames before the day, not on it.** Hispanic Heritage Month opens Tue Sep 15. Suicide Prevention Month is all month. World Alzheimer's Day is Mon Sep 21. Every one of these should be posted at least a week ahead, because you are talking to the person who *runs* the campaign, not the person who joins it.
- **Turn every post into an outreach hook.** Post the suicide prevention frame, then DM ten orgs running suicide prevention campaigns with "made this, it is free, want your logo on it." The post is what makes the DM credible.
- **The offer, stated the same way every time:** a free custom frame with their logo, built by you, no strings, no attribution required. That is a genuinely good offer and it should sound like one.
- **Optional but high value:** when the post-download screen ships in week 6, record it and post the clip. "Here is the moment a supporter becomes an organizer."

**September exit criteria:** at least three real organizations running campaigns, a viral coefficient you can actually name, and a donate button you have mentioned once without making it weird.

### October — primary job: ride the traffic

October is the biggest profile-frame month of the year. Breast Cancer Awareness Month opens Thu Oct 1. World Mental Health Day is Sat Oct 10. Unity Day is Wed Oct 21. Domestic Violence Awareness runs all month.

- **Front-load everything.** Your October posts should be written and scheduled by Sep 27. When traffic arrives you want to be answering DMs and watching conversion, not writing captions.
- **Cadence goes up to 3 a week on LinkedIn for October only.** This is the one month where the extra volume is justified.
- **Weekend coverage matters this month.** Oct 10 is a Saturday. Schedule it.
- **Post the numbers as they happen.** "1,400 people added a pink frame this week" is the best post you will make all quarter, and you can only make it in October.
- **Start Giving Tuesday outreach mid-month.** Dec 1 pages are already seeded. Orgs plan Giving Tuesday in October, not November.

**October exit criteria:** a case study drafted from your best campaign, a real answer on whether social drove any measurable signups, and a decision on whether to keep going into Q4.

## Post templates

Written in brand voice already. Short sentences, second person, no em dashes, no buzzwords.

**Frame drop**
> [Day] is [date].
>
> We made a frame for it. It is free, there is no signup, and your supporters will never see an ad.
>
> Add your photo in about ten seconds: [link]

**Campaign spotlight**
> Nobody told us about this one.
>
> [Org] found Ollabs on their own and ran a campaign for [thing]. [N] people are wearing it right now.
>
> That is the whole idea. One frame, one link, everybody shows up wearing the same thing.

**Position**
> Twibbon charges your supporters to remove a watermark.
>
> We think that is backwards. The person donating their profile picture to your cause is already giving you something.
>
> On Ollabs supporters never pay, never see an ad, and never get a watermark. If we ever need money, we will ask the organizations with a budget, not the people showing up.

**Build in public**
> Shipped this week: [thing].
>
> [One sentence on why it mattered.]
>
> [One honest sentence on what was harder than expected.]
>
> Building Ollabs in public. It is a free, ad-free way to run a profile picture campaign.

**Outreach DM** (not a post, but this is where the posts pay off)
> Hi [name], I built a free tool called Ollabs for running profile picture campaigns. No ads, no signup, supporters never pay.
>
> I noticed [org] is doing [thing] for [month]. Happy to build you a frame with your logo, free, no strings. Takes me about ten minutes and you keep the link forever.
>
> Example: [link to a live campaign]

## One thing to watch on the visual side

You now have two visual systems and they should not be confused.

- **The product frames** are restrained circular rings, designed to survive at 32 pixels. That restraint is correct and should not change.
- **The feed graphics** are the loud sticker style from the girlfriend-day frame. Chunky type, hearts, offset shadows, high contrast. That is right for a feed, where the job is to stop a thumb.

Keep them separate. Loud in the feed, quiet on the photo. The recolored brand version of the girlfriend-day frame is your template for the loud one.

Also: that frame is dated Aug 1, which has passed. Treat it as the style reference and the 2027 asset, not something to publish this week.

## What to measure

Three numbers, checked monthly. Social gets judged on assist, not on last click.

| Metric | Where | What good looks like by Oct 31 |
|---|---|---|
| **Referral sessions from social** | GA4, `utm_medium=social` | Any consistent signal at all. This will be small and that is expected. |
| **Outreach reply rate** | Your own count | 20%+ on DMs. If it is under 10%, the profile is not doing its job, so fix the profile before sending more DMs. |
| **Campaigns you can trace to a conversation** | Manual | 3+ across the quarter. This is the real number. One university campaign is worth more than 10,000 impressions. |

Do not track followers. It will make you optimize for the wrong thing.

---

## What not to do

- **Do not post five times a week to start.** You will stop, and an abandoned feed reads worse than a quiet one.
- **Do not build a content calendar past October.** You do not know yet whether any of this works.
- **Do not point Madak clients at Ollabs to seed campaigns.** Your launch plan already flagged this and the reasoning still holds. Keep the venture clean.
- **Do not let social eat the outreach hours.** If you have one hour and a choice between writing a post and sending five DMs, send the DMs. Every time.
